About us

Most people learn money the hard way. We teach it in fifth grade.

After the first overdraft or the first scam call is too late. So students teach students, peer to peer, in words that stick, and then help protect their grandparents too.

Read our story
Our mission

To make financial education and leadership free for every generation. Students learn real-world money and leadership skills, and seniors learn to protect themselves from scams.

Our vision

A future where every generation is financially empowered: young people build lifelong money skills, seniors are protected from financial scams, and communities grow stronger and more confident.

Programs

Three labs. One idea.

Learn it before you need it. Every lab is interactive, led by trained student facilitators, and measured with a short survey before and after.

01 · Save

Financial Literacy Lab

Build money skills for life.

Hands-on activities that make budgeting, saving, spending and investing easy to understand, taught at the right level for each grade.

Grades 5–8

  • Needs vs. wants
  • A budget with the 50/30/20 rule
  • Savings goals and thoughtful spending
  • A first look at investing

Grades 9–12

  • The stock market and investing basics
  • Compound growth, and why starting early matters
  • Credit scores, debt and first paychecks
  • The real cost of college

The takeawayPractical money skills students can use today for a stronger tomorrow.

03 · Protect

Scam Shield Lab

Spot scams. Stay safe.

Led by student facilitators, seniors learn to recognize common fraud tactics, protect their personal information and respond safely.

Spot the signs

Phishing emails, suspicious texts and fraudulent calls.

Catch imposters

Callers posing as the government, a bank or a family member.

Bank and pay safely

Safer habits for online banking and digital payments.

Know what to do

The steps to take, and who to contact, if a scam is suspected.

The takeawayThe warning signs to watch for, and safer decisions online and over the phone.

Our story

Started at 14, in eighth grade.

Nevaan Chandak saw how missing a few money basics could turn into real hardship, and that budgeting and saving were being taught too late, if at all.

So he built his own workshops, taught the first ones himself and trained classmates to teach too. Every session is still free, because a family's income shouldn't decide who gets to learn this.

  • 2025First workshop, taught by Nevaan in eighth grade
  • 4,000+Students reached through peer-led sessions
